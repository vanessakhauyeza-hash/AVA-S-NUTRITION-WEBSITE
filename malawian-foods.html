MALAWI FOOD GUIDE

Malawian Foods & Nutrition

Malawi has a wide variety of foods that can contribute to a varied and balanced diet. This guide introduces familiar foods and shows how different food groups can contribute to everyday meals.

There is no single meal that is perfect for everyone. Nutrition needs vary according to age, activity, health status, culture, food availability and other individual circumstances.

01 • STAPLE FOODS

Staples and energy-giving foods

Staple foods provide an important source of energy and are common components of meals in Malawi. Examples include maize-based foods, rice, potatoes, sweet potatoes and cassava.

🌽

Maize

Maize is widely consumed in Malawi and can be prepared in different forms, including nsima.

🍚

Rice

Rice is another staple food that can be combined with vegetables, legumes or animal-source foods.

🍠

Sweet potatoes

Sweet potatoes are starchy root foods that can be included as part of varied meals.

🥔

Potatoes

Potatoes are another locally familiar starchy food that can contribute energy to meals.

02 • PROTEIN FOODS

Legumes, fish, eggs and other protein foods

Protein foods provide protein and can also contribute other nutrients. A varied diet can include both plant-based and animal-source foods.

🫘

Beans

Beans and other legumes can contribute protein and dietary fibre to meals.

🥜

Groundnuts

Groundnuts are nutrient-dense foods that provide protein and fats and can be included in a varied diet.

🐟

Fish

Fish can provide protein and, depending on the type of fish, other important nutrients.

🥚

Eggs

Eggs are an animal-source food that provides protein and several vitamins and minerals.

03 • VEGETABLES

Vegetables and leafy greens

Vegetables can contribute vitamins, minerals, fibre and other beneficial components of the diet. Including a variety of vegetables can help increase dietary diversity.

🥬

Leafy vegetables

Locally available leafy vegetables can form an important part of meals and contribute micronutrients and fibre.

🥕

Other vegetables

Including different types and colours of vegetables can help increase variety within the diet.

04 • FRUIT

Fruits

Fruits are an important part of a varied diet and provide vitamins, minerals, fibre and naturally occurring sugars.

🍌

Bananas

Bananas are a familiar fruit that can be eaten on their own or included as part of meals and snacks.

🥭

Mangoes

Mangoes are a locally familiar fruit and can contribute vitamins and other nutrients.

🍊

Other fruits

Where available, include a variety of seasonal fruits as part of a diverse eating pattern.

05 • BUILDING A MEAL

How can you build a varied Malawian meal?

A healthy diet is not about one specific food. WHO describes healthy diets using principles including adequacy, balance, moderation and diversity. The exact combination can vary according to local foods, culture and individual circumstances.

A simple way to think about your plate

  • Include a staple food such as nsima, rice, potatoes or sweet potatoes.
  • Add a protein-rich food such as beans, peas, fish, eggs or another protein source.
  • Include vegetables where available.
  • Include fruit as part of your overall eating pattern.
  • Aim for variety across meals rather than relying on only one type of food.

06 • PRACTICAL NUTRITION

Eating well with locally available foods

Healthy eating does not have to mean choosing expensive or imported foods. The foods available to a household depend on season, location, income, food prices, culture and other factors.

A practical approach is to make use of a variety of nutritious foods that are available and affordable, while aiming for diversity across food groups.
